Our story

We’ve had a few setups

For me, it started with heading west for work in some of the remotest parts of Queensland and the Northern Territory. A swag beside the ute, under a tarp strung between trees, or tucked under the trailer tray when it was wet. No electric fridge or gas shower back then. You ate the food that needed to stay cold first, and when the beer got warm, it was time for more ice on the next trip to town. Who remembers using dry ice?

Then came the family, and the swags made way for tents, then camper trailers. We worked our way from a box trailer with a tent to a pretty flash setup. Sometimes I wonder if going camping was partly an excuse to try new gear. Every trip brought more tinkering and a list of mods for when we got home. I’m still just as obsessed today.

Eventually, the long setups, pack-ups and wet canvas got old. We moved to a big off-road caravan with bunks and a tow rig big enough to pull every gadget we could fit inside. But for us, sitting in the air conditioning while the kids were on their iPads, we started to miss the simplicity of being outside.

Then the kids outgrew the bunks and got busy doing their own thing. What suited us then isn’t what suits us now.

Less chaos in our camping

These days, our perfect setup is a canopy on the ute. We throw the food in and go. We’ve come full circle. The kids are getting their own gear now, and camping is still a family thing. Free camps and remote spots are our go-to: somewhere quiet to pull up, settle in and switch off.

We’re still mad keen hikers and always up for an adventure. We just don’t think setting up camp needs to be hard work.

Years of working out bush

For over twenty years, our daily grind was mining contracting, doing rehabilitation, exploration and site work in some of the harshest environments. Harvesting native seed took our teams bush for weeks at a time. Remote camps were part of daily life.

With a fleet of earthmoving equipment, our own workshop and field maintenance teams, we learnt what holds up, what breaks when it shakes and what’s practical to maintain. We also learnt how to set up utes for work and still camp out of them.

When something breaks out there, it’s rarely a quick trip to the shop. That experience shapes how we choose our camping gear.

Gear has to earn its keep

Like most campers, we’ve bought our share of cheap gear from the big outdoor retailers. We’ve binned plenty of broken awnings and other casualties after Easter, too.

We’ve had fridges come unplugged on corrugations, gear rub through and welds break. We’ve forgotten the gas bottle and left the tent pegs behind. Nothing like opening a kid’s tent full of black mould and making a 700 km round trip to buy another one. Ask me how I know.

We know everyone starts somewhere. There’s no single right setup, and it doesn’t all have to happen at once. GTW Outback is about choosing practical, durable gear worth keeping. If we wouldn’t use it, we won’t sell it.

Experience shapes our range

GTW Outback brings our working experience together with our love of the bush. We care about how gear is built, how it fits together and how it works a long way from home.

Our brand may be new, but the idea is simple: make the whole rig work better. We’re bringing together a canopy system inside and out — modular storage, rooftop tents, improved ladders, awnings and shower boxes, with each part selected to work with the others. Easier access, less hunting for gear and less work when you pull up at camp.

Start with the canopy you already own or choose a complete setup. Build it one part at a time or all at once, then rearrange your storage as work, family and camping needs change. You deal with one supplier and have one place to turn for support.

We’re bringing the expertise of qualified tradespeople and engineers into the testing and selection of our range, and working with manufacturing partners to have our gear made to our requirements. Every product needs to meet the standards we’re prepared to put our name on.

From our family to yours

Whether you’re setting up a ute for work, a full-time camp rig or something in between, we want you to feel your investment in quality was worthwhile.

That includes support from real people who share your love of getting out there. When you need help, our no-fuss promise is a straightforward conversation with someone who understands why your gear matters.
